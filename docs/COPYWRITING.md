# 1. Purpose

This document defines the editorial standards for every piece of written communication produced by Desert Fox Digital.

It establishes a consistent voice across the website, service pages, case studies, journal articles, emails, landing pages, forms, and future digital products.

The objective is not simply consistency.

It is to ensure every word reinforces the Desert Fox Digital brand.

If another content document conflicts with this one, this document takes precedence.

---

# 2. Editorial Philosophy

Desert Fox Digital does not write like a marketing agency.

It writes like an editorial studio.

Every sentence should communicate confidence through clarity rather than persuasion through pressure.

The writing should feel considered, restrained, and intentional.

Readers should finish a page feeling informed—not sold to.

Our writing prioritizes:

- Clarity
- Honesty
- Simplicity
- Precision
- Thoughtfulness
- Long-term value

Not:

- Hype
- Urgency
- Exaggeration
- Buzzwords
- Manipulation

---

The Desert Fox Digital voice is:

# 4. Writing Principles

## 4.1 Clarity Before Cleverness

Every sentence should be understood on the first read.

Avoid writing that exists simply to sound impressive.

If a simpler sentence communicates the same idea, choose the simpler sentence.

---

## 4.2 Educate Before Selling

Teach first.

Invite second.

Pages should create understanding before presenting a call to action.

Visitors should never feel rushed toward a decision.

---

## 4.3 Precision Over Quantity

Say only what needs to be said.

Avoid repetition.

Remove filler words.

Each paragraph should communicate one clear idea.

---

## 4.4 Show, Don't Inflate

Support claims with:

- Process
- Examples
- Results
- Case studies
- Clear explanations

Avoid unsupported superlatives.

Instead of:

> We're the best.

Write:

> Every recommendation is grounded in research, measurable outcomes, and long-term strategy.

---

## 4.5 Long-Term Thinking

Write for longevity.

Avoid language tied to temporary trends or short-lived marketing terminology.

The content should still feel relevant years after publication.

---

# 5. Tone

The overall tone should feel like an experienced advisor.

Not a salesperson.

Imagine explaining your work across a table to a thoughtful business owner.

The conversation is calm.

Questions are welcomed.

Confidence comes from experience rather than persuasion.

Readers should feel:

- Understood
- Informed
- Respected
- Encouraged

Never pressured.

---

# 6. Writing Style

### Sentences

Prefer short to medium-length sentences.

Mix sentence lengths naturally to create rhythm.

Avoid unnecessarily complex structures.

---

### Paragraphs

Keep paragraphs concise.

Most paragraphs should contain two to four sentences.

Long-form content may occasionally use longer paragraphs where appropriate.

---

### Reading Level

Aim for plain, professional language.

Explain technical concepts without oversimplifying them.

Assume the reader is intelligent but not necessarily a marketing expert.

---

### Active Voice

Prefer active voice whenever possible.

Good:

> We build websites that help local businesses grow.

Avoid:

> Websites are built with the intention of facilitating growth.

---

# 7. Messaging Priorities

Every page should answer, in order:

1. What is this?
2. Why does it matter?
3. How does Desert Fox Digital approach it differently?
4. What outcome becomes possible?
5. What should the visitor do next?

Never assume the reader already understands the value of the service.

Explain before asking.

---

# 8. Headlines

Headlines communicate ideas.

Not keywords.

Good headlines are:

- Clear
- Memorable
- Specific
- Human

Maximum length:

Approximately 12 words.

Whenever possible:

Two lines or fewer.

Examples:

- Mark Your Territory.
- Grow with purpose.
- Marketing works better when every piece has a purpose.
- Build a foundation before you build momentum.
- Visibility begins with clarity.

Avoid:

- We Deliver Comprehensive Digital Marketing Solutions
- Unlock Explosive Growth
- Your Success Starts Here
- Results That Speak for Themselves

---

# 9. Supporting Copy

Supporting paragraphs should expand the headline.

Not repeat it.

Maximum reading width:

Approximately 70 characters per line.

Preferred length:

Two to four sentences.

Example:

Desert Fox Digital helps local businesses build a stronger presence through thoughtful strategy, search visibility, and digital experiences designed to last.

---

# 10. Microcopy

Microcopy should remove uncertainty.

Examples include:

- Navigation labels
- Button text
- Form labels
- Error messages
- Success confirmations
- Empty states
- Helper text

Characteristics:

- Brief
- Direct
- Friendly
- Unambiguous

Good microcopy answers questions before users need to ask them.

---

# 11. Calls to Action

Calls to action should feel like invitations.

Never pressure the reader.

Never create artificial urgency.

The visitor should feel confident taking the next step.

Preferred characteristics:

- Clear
- Specific
- Action-oriented
- Calm

Preferred length:

**2–3 words maximum.** This aligns with the button copy limit defined in UI-PATTERNS.md and COMPONENTS.md. Four-word labels are not permitted.

Examples:

- Start a Project
- Claim Your Ground
- Explore Services
- View Our Work
- Read the Journal
- Learn More
- Book a Call
- Contact Us

Avoid:

- Buy Now
- Act Fast
- Limited Time
- Get Yours Today
- Click Here
- Don't Miss Out

---

# 12. Navigation Labels

Navigation should use familiar language.

Preferred labels:

- Work
- Services
- About
- Journal
- Contact

Primary CTA:

Start a Project

Avoid creative navigation labels that reduce clarity.

---

# 13. Button Copy Standards

Buttons describe what happens next.

**Maximum: 3 words.** This limit is enforced across UI-PATTERNS.md (content limits table), COMPONENTS.md (#6 Primary Button content rules), and this document. It is not a guideline—it is a hard limit.

Good

Start a Project

View Project

Explore Services

Read Article

Contact Us

Bad

Submit

Continue

Go

Click Here

Next

Button copy should remove ambiguity.

---

# 14. Form Language

Forms should reduce anxiety.

Explain why information is requested.

Labels remain visible.

Never rely on placeholders alone.

Preferred labels:

Name

Business

Email Address

Phone (Optional)

Service Needed

Tell us about your project

Supporting copy example:

Tell us a little about your business, your goals, and any challenges you're currently facing.

---

# 15. Error Messages

Error messages should be helpful.

Never blame the user.

Good

Please enter a valid email address.

This field is required.

Please select a service.

Something went wrong while sending your message. Please try again.

Avoid

Invalid input.

Error 400.

Submission failed.

Incorrect.

---

# 16. Success Messages

Success messages should reassure the visitor.

Example

## Thanks for reaching out.

We've received your message and will review it shortly.

You can expect to hear from us within two business days.

Avoid exaggerated enthusiasm.

---

# 17. SEO Copywriting

Write for people first.

Search engines second.

Every page should include:

- One H1
- Logical heading hierarchy
- Descriptive headings
- Descriptive links
- Natural keyword usage

Never force keywords into sentences.

Good writing improves SEO.

# 23. Numbers & Statistics

Use numbers carefully.

Always provide context.

Good

Organic traffic increased by 214% over twelve months.

Bad

214%

Numbers should support a narrative.

Never exist by themselves.

---

# 24. Capitalization

Use sentence case for:

Headings

Buttons

Navigation

Forms

Avoid excessive title case.

Good

Bad

Grow With Purpose

# 25. Punctuation

Use punctuation sparingly.

Prefer periods over exclamation marks.

Avoid multiple punctuation marks.

Never use:

!!!

???

Excessive ellipses...

Use em dashes sparingly.

# 36. Implementation Framework

**This section resolves the conflict between the role brief and the full documentation set.**

## Authoritative Tech Stack

The Desert Fox Digital website is built on **Next.js 15 (App Router)** with **TypeScript** in strict mode.

This is established across the following authoritative documents, all at Production status:

- README.md — "Framework: Next.js 14+" (superseded by later docs; 15 is current)
- BUILD-CHECKLIST.md — "Framework: Next.js 15 (App Router)"
- BUILD-ROADMAP.md — "Framework: Next.js 15 (App Router)"
- IMPLEMENTATION-STANDARDS.md — "Framework: Next.js 15 (App Router)"
- SITE-STRUCTURE.md — full directory tree assumes App Router conventions (`src/app/`, `layout.tsx`, `page.tsx`, `route.ts`)

## What This Means for Implementation

- All components are TypeScript React components, not Vanilla HTML files.
- Styles are CSS Cascade Layers loaded globally — no CSS Modules, no inline styles.
- Routing follows Next.js App Router conventions (`src/app/(marketing)/`, `src/app/api/`).
- Server Components are the default. `'use client'` is added only when hooks, state, or browser APIs require it.
- The contact API route is `src/app/api/contact/route.ts` using Resend for email delivery.
- Fonts are loaded via `next/font` and self-hosted in `public/fonts/`.
- Images use `next/image` throughout.

## Role Brief Conflict — Resolved

The implementation role brief referenced "Vanilla HTML5, CSS, and JavaScript (ES Modules)" and stated not to introduce Next.js without explicit approval.

That instruction conflicts with the full production documentation set. The documentation set is the higher authority. The Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS constraint is superseded.

**All implementation work follows Next.js 15 App Router.** No Vanilla HTML output should be produced for production deliverables.
